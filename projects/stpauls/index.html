01 Our Story

From Nau Meel
to Iqbal Shaheed Road

St. Paul’s began in 1940 as a modest primary school run by the Franciscan Missionaries at Nau Meel (Kala Pul), serving the educational needs of the neighbourhood’s poor. In its first twelve nomadic years the school moved through the Braganza Building, Younis Building on Frere Street, and rooms borrowed from St. Patrick’s Cathedral — before the Catholic Church secured the present site, a former British cemetery yard, and built its first ten-room schoolhouse.

In 1952, St. Paul’s sent up its first batch for the S.S.C. Examination — achieving a cent-percent result in the process.

Building a
Living Legacy

Under Rev. Fr. Armando Trinidade (1965–1973), the school opened its Cambridge Section and a nursery class, and added a second storey. Rev. Fr. Max Rodrigues (1984–2000) introduced computer classes and audio-visual learning, and led the school through its Golden Jubilee in 1991. Since 2000, Rev. Fr. Anthony Martis has added science and computer laboratories, a music room, an art room, and new teaching wings — carrying the mission forward for a new generation of Paulians.

04

Life on Campus

A Campus Built by Hand

Principal Fr. Fulgence Kager was architect, teacher and clerk in one — leading staff and students to clear the grounds, plant trees, and lay the foundations of the school that stands today on Iqbal (S.J) Shaheed Road.

Faith, Discipline, Belonging

From Silver Jubilarians on staff to Mrs. Silvia Pinto’s fifty years of teaching, St. Paul’s has always been defined by the people who stayed — building a family atmosphere that generations of Paulians still return to visit.

Growing, Wing by Wing

Rev. Fr. Anthony Martis’s new wing of twenty classrooms continues a tradition of expansion that has run since 1940 — each generation of leadership adding the rooms, labs and studios the next needs.
